## Purpose and Supported Applications

Given a candidate servo motor's own catalog rating data (rated/peak torque,
rated speed, rotor inertia), an optional gearbox (reusing `screw.gear_ratio`
directly, plus a required transmission efficiency whenever geared), an
optional drive's own regenerative-energy absorption capacity, and an
optional holding brake's own rated torque, plus the drive torque and
rotational context already resolved by `ball-screw` (Unit 4.3) and
`motion-profile` (Unit 4.2), this module checks whether that specific servo
motor is mechanically viable for the axis: total reflected system inertia
and inertia ratio, RMS (effective) torque against a required margin, peak
(maximum momentary) torque against a required margin, operating speed
against the motor's own rated speed, and regenerative energy released
against the drive's own absorption capacity (when supplied). It reports a
required-spec / pass-fail-with-margin result for a servo motor the engineer
has already identified by model — it does not search a catalog and rank
candidates.

## Validity Envelope and Assumptions

- One candidate servo motor per calculation run; does not search a catalog
  or rank candidates.
- Only the `normal` and `peak` load cases, matching every other Milestone 4
  module's own scope restriction.
- `drive.reflected_load_inertia` is a required engineer-supplied input — no
  released upstream parameter derives it.
- The RMS-torque margin, peak-torque margin, and maximum inertia ratio have
  no built-in default, even though this project's own sources disagree
  three-to-five ways on each — including on the margin's own value: Omron's
  own worked example uses a flat `0.8` for both, while THK's own two worked
  examples use `1.0` (a plain "at least" minimum, no derating).
- The effective (RMS) torque formula relies on a closed-cycle assumption
  (total system inertia and the per-case load torque both stay constant
  across a cycle). Verified as an algebraic identity against a synthetic
  per-phase torque profile (`closed-cycle-benchmark.ts`) and against
  Omron's own and THK's own horizontal worked examples (both closely
  match). THK's own vertical worked example is a real, sourced counter-case
  where the precondition does not hold — see "Tolerances and Deviations"
  below.
- Regenerative energy assumes 100% of the released kinetic energy reaches
  the drive's own absorption path — no drive-electronics efficiency loss or
  DC-bus capacitor-absorption credit is modeled.
- Gearbox backlash, transmission error, torsional rigidity, and mechanical
  life are reported catalog values only, not evaluated.
- Holding-brake rated torque is a reported catalog value only — no
  standalone catalog-comparison check exists.
- Drive/amplifier current and voltage compatibility are out of scope
  entirely — the unit registry has no electrical-current dimension yet.
- A case with exactly zero total system inertia, motor rotor inertia,
  lead, or gear ratio is unsupported: `compute` throws rather than
  reporting a degenerate result.

## Reference Examples (Published Worked Examples)

### Example 1 — Omron R88M-U20030, direct-connected ball-screw axis

- Source: `jp.omron.servo_motor_selection_guide@csm-tg-e-3-1`, "Technical
  Guide for Servo Motor Selection" sample calculation, printed pp. 12-13
- Inputs: 5 kg load, 10 mm lead, no gearbox, OMNUC U-series motor
  R88M-U20030
- Published/expected result: `T_A = 0.165 N*m`, `T1 = 0.173 N*m`,
  `Trms = 0.0828 N*m`; every applicable check (load inertia, effective
  torque, maximum momentary torque, maximum rotation speed) passes;
  regenerative energy explicitly omitted by Omron's own example
- MachineStudio result: reproduced through `executeModule`
  (`lib/modules/drive-train/0.1.0/omron-reference-example.ts`)
- Difference: acceleration torque and maximum momentary torque within
  `0.001 N*m`; effective (RMS) torque within `0.0003 N*m` (~0.25%) —
  `rms_acceleration` is not printed directly by Omron and is derived here
  from Omron's own printed duty-cycle segments, so the residual reflects
  Omron's own 3-significant-figure intermediate rounding, not slack
  introduced to pass the test
- Tolerance applied and why: `0.001 N*m` on torque figures Omron prints to
  3 decimal places; `0.0003 N*m` (~0.25%) on the derived RMS figure,
  accounting for the intermediate-rounding chain
- Pass / fail against tolerance: **pass**

### Example 2 — THK horizontal, WTF2040-2 ball-screw axis

- Source: `jp.thk.example_ball_screw_selection@technico-mirror-2026-08-10`,
  "High-speed Transfer Equipment (Horizontal Use)" worked example, "Studying
  the Driving Motor" section
- Inputs: 80 kg total load, 40 mm lead, no gearbox, AC servo motor (no
  catalog SKU named by THK)
- Published/expected result: `Tk = 4730 N*mm`, `Trms = 1305 N*mm`; motor
  inertia `>= reflected load inertia / 10`
- MachineStudio result: reproduced through `executeModule`
  (`lib/modules/drive-train/0.1.0/thk-reference-examples.ts`)
- Difference: maximum momentary torque within `0.05 N*m` (~0.3%); effective
  (RMS) torque within `0.005 N*m` (~0.06%) — both residuals traced
  precisely to THK's own printed 3-significant-figure angular-acceleration
  rounding (`1050 rad/s^2` printed vs. `1047.2 rad/s^2` exact), not slack
  introduced to pass the test. THK's own inertia-ratio rule reproduces
  exactly.
- Tolerance applied and why: `0.05 N*m` (~0.3%) and `0.005 N*m` (~0.06%) —
  both accounting for THK's own printed angular-acceleration rounding
- Pass / fail against tolerance: **pass**

### Example 3 — THK vertical, BLK1510 ball-screw axis (partial)

- Source: same document as Example 2, "Vertical Conveyance System" worked
  example
- Inputs: 50 kg total load, 10 mm lead, no gearbox, AC servo motor (no
  catalog SKU named); asymmetric per-direction load torque (900 N*mm
  upward, 830 N*mm downward) and a nonzero stationary holding torque
  (658 N*mm)
- Published/expected result: `Tk1 = 1100 N*mm` (governing upward
  direction), `Tk2 = 630 N*mm` (gravity-assisted downward), motor inertia
  `>= reflected load inertia / 10`, `Trms = 743 N*mm`
- MachineStudio result: reproduced through `executeModule` for the
  governing upward/peak scenario only — a deliberate partial reproduction,
  the same treatment KTR's own example receives in `coupling 0.1.0`'s own
  validation record when a source does not supply everything this module's
  compute path needs
- Difference: maximum momentary torque within `0.05 N*m` (~0.4%) of
  `Tk1 = 1100 N*mm`; inertia-ratio rule exact. Effective (RMS) torque is
  **deliberately not reproduced** — this module's own computed value
  (~0.901 N*m) diverges from THK's own printed `Trms = 743 N*mm` by
  roughly 21%, a real, quantified deviation (see "Tolerances and
  Deviations" below), not a rounding residual. THK's own lower,
  gravity-assisted downward momentary torque (`Tk2 = 630 N*mm`) is not
  reproducible by this module at all — see "Tolerances and Deviations."
- Tolerance applied and why: `0.05 N*m` (~0.4%) on the momentary-torque
  figure this module does reproduce; no tolerance applies to the RMS-torque
  figure, since it is explicitly not claimed as reproduced
- Pass / fail against tolerance: **pass** (for the momentary-torque and
  inertia-ratio figures actually reproduced)

## Independent Method or Tool Comparison

**Satisfied (2026-08-10).**
`lib/modules/drive-train/0.1.0/closed-cycle-benchmark.ts` implements a
structurally different, direct per-phase computation of RMS torque as a
genuine second computation — the roadmap's Definition of Done item 9.

- Independent method/tool: a direct per-phase RMS-torque computation,
  `Trms = sqrt(sum(T_i^2*t_i)/sum(t_i))` — the general shape every Stage 1
  source gives (`context/modules/drive-train/stage-1-spec.md` item 3),
  applied straight to synthetic multi-phase torque profiles rather than
  derived from aggregate `rms_acceleration`/load-torque statistics. This
  project's own derivation, not sourced from a manufacturer.
- Case(s) compared: four repeating-cycle shapes (2-phase symmetric, 2-phase
  asymmetric, 4-phase Voss-style accel/dwell/decel/dwell, 5-phase
  multi-sign-change) crossed with four `(J_total, T_load)` magnitude pairs
- Result from independent method: the direct per-phase computation
- MachineStudio result: `resolveEffectiveTorque`'s closed form
- Difference and explanation: matches to floating-point precision across
  all sixteen combinations — an algebraic identity, not an approximate
  corroboration, the same treatment `lib/modules/support-bearing/0.1.0/
  nsk-fh-benchmark.ts` gives NSK's own `fh` method. A counter-example (a
  non-repeating cycle, net velocity change `!= 0`) demonstrates the two
  methods diverge by a large, physically meaningful margin once the
  repeating-cycle precondition is violated — proving that precondition,
  not just the formula's arithmetic, is what the identity actually depends
  on.

## Tolerances and Deviations

- The gearbox transmission-efficiency derating this module applies on top
  of `screw.drive_torque` is not present in `ball-screw 0.1.0`'s own
  released kernel — a real, documented gap in an already-released module,
  not invented here (`context/modules/drive-train/stage-1-spec.md` "A Real
  Gap Found in an Already-Released Kernel").
- `drive.acceleration_torque` uses the larger-magnitude of
  `motion.profile.peak_acceleration`/`peak_deceleration` as a single
  conservative figure rather than evaluating both phases separately — a
  documented generalization of Omron's own symmetric worked example,
  proved (not just asserted) to bound the true worst-case single-phase
  torque.
- THK's own vertical worked example (Example 3 above) is a real, sourced
  case where the closed-cycle RMS-torque assumption's own precondition
  genuinely does not hold: its load torque differs between the upward
  (900 N*mm) and downward (830 N*mm) halves of the duty cycle instead of
  staying constant, and its stationary phase carries a nonzero holding
  torque (658 N*mm) rather than zero or the constant load torque. Feeding
  this module's own real required inputs for the governing (upward) case
  through the actual closed-form kernel gives an effective torque about
  21% above THK's own printed figure — a genuine, quantified deviation,
  not a rounding residual (contrast Example 2's own <0.1% residual). This
  module also cannot express THK's own lower, gravity-assisted downward
  momentary torque (630 N*mm) at all, since `resolveAccelerationTorque`
  always adds the acceleration torque to the load torque rather than
  modeling a direction where gravity assists — a deliberate, documented
  conservative choice, not a defect. Both are real scope limits for
  vertical/asymmetric-holding installations worth revisiting in a future
  version, not worked around here.

## Unsupported Conditions

- `holding` and `emergency_stop` load cases — only `normal` and `peak` are
  resolved.
- Drive/amplifier current and voltage compatibility — no electrical-current
  dimension exists in the unit registry yet.
- A standalone holding-brake torque check — no source gives a
  catalog-comparison formula; `drive.brake_rated_torque` is reported only.
- Gearbox backlash, transmission error, torsional rigidity, and mechanical
  life checks — only qualitative catalog ranges exist by gearbox family;
  not modeled as a check.
- Torque/speed-curve comparison — checks compare scalar rated/peak torque
  and rated speed, not a curve-valued capability.
- Vertical/asymmetric-holding installations where the closed-cycle
  RMS-torque precondition does not hold — see Example 3 and "Tolerances and
  Deviations" above.
- Drive-electronics efficiency loss or DC-bus capacitor-absorption credit
  in the regenerative-energy check — 100% absorption is assumed.
- A case with exactly zero total system inertia, motor rotor inertia, lead,
  or gear ratio — `compute` throws rather than reporting a degenerate
  result.
