## Purpose and Supported Applications

Given a candidate ball-screw shaft's own geometry and catalog rating data,
plus the axial load cases and motion timing already resolved by
`axis-load-cases` (Unit 4.1) and `motion-profile` (Unit 4.2), this module
checks whether that specific screw is mechanically viable for the axis:
static safety under the required load, fatigue (nominal) life against the
duty cycle, clearance from buckling under compressive load, and clearance
from critical (whip) speed. It reports a required-spec / pass-fail-with-
margin result for a screw the engineer has already identified by size, lead,
and end-support arrangement — it does not search a catalog and rank
candidates (`context/modules/ball-screw/stage-1-spec.md` "Purpose").

## Validity Envelope and Assumptions

- One straight ball-screw shaft on one linear axis, consuming exactly one
  resolved axial-load case at a time from `axis-load-cases`. `0.1.0`
  supports only the `normal`/`peak` cases — `holding`/`emergency_stop` are
  not implemented (matching `axis-load-cases 0.1.0`'s own scope).
- Rotating-screw / translating-nut arrangement only; the rotating-nut
  arrangement is out of scope.
- One of four end-support arrangements: fixed-fixed, fixed-supported,
  supported-supported, fixed-free.
- Buckling and critical-speed formulas use the screw's minor (root)
  diameter, not the nominal/major diameter.
- The dynamic load rating must be on a revolutions basis; the input schema
  rejects a distance-basis rating outright rather than silently
  misconverting it (a real cross-catalog incompatibility found during
  sourcing — Rockford's own catalog rates screws against `10^6` inches of
  travel, not `10^6` revolutions).
- The static safety factor minimum and the buckling safety margin are
  **required module inputs with no built-in default** — published guidance
  disagrees on both (see "Tolerances and Deviations" below) and no source
  met this project's evidence bar for either.
- No preload-dependent stiffness modeling beyond the internal-friction/
  efficiency terms in the drive-torque formula, no thermal derating beyond
  the ambient-temperature context `axis-load-cases` already carries, no
  structural compliance, backlash, or lubrication-regime modeling.

Full detail: `context/modules/ball-screw/stage-1-spec.md` "Validity
Envelope (Proposed)"; `lib/modules/ball-screw/0.1.0/manifest.ts`
`validityEnvelopeSummary`.

## Reference Examples (Published Worked Examples)

Six examples are reproduced, from two independent manufacturers (Rockford,

Recorded honestly per that distinction; see `lib/modules/ball-screw/0.1.0/

### Example 1 — Rockford drive torque

- Source: Rockford Ball Screw, "How To Size A Ball Screw," step 10
- Inputs: `Pt = 500 lbf`, `Sl = .250 in`, `Eff = 90%`
- Published/expected result: `23 in-lbs` (the source's own shown arithmetic,
  `.177*500*.250`, computes to `22.125`, not `23` — its own final rounding
  is internally inconsistent, not evidence against the formula)
- MachineStudio result: `22.1 in-lbs` (`resolveDriveTorque`, using the more
  precise `1/(2*pi*0.9)` instead of the source's rounded `.177`)
- Difference: matches the source's own shown arithmetic (not its rounded
  print) to within `0.1 in-lbs`
- Tolerance applied and why: `±0.1 in-lbs` — catalog-rounding tolerance,
  checked against the source's own shown work rather than its inconsistent
  final print
- Pass / fail: **pass**

## Independent Method or Tool Comparison

Three independent comparisons, covering every check in this module:

**1. Drive torque — three-manufacturer agreement.** Implemented from
Oriental Motor's "Motor Sizing Calculations"
(`T_L = F*P/(2*pi*eta) + mu0*F0*P/(2*pi)`, divided by gear ratio) and
independently cross-checked against two other manufacturers' own worked
examples: Rockford Ball Screw (Example 1 above) and THK (Example 4 above).
Both agree with the primary formula to within their own catalog-rounding
precision.

**2. Buckling and critical speed — THK independent benchmark
(`lib/modules/ball-screw/0.1.0/thk-benchmark.ts`).** `math.ts` implements
Rockford Ball Screw's own formula and end-fixity coefficients directly
(inch/lbf units, `Fe * 14,030,000 * (Dmin^4/L^2)` for buckling). THK's own
catalog page (A-744/A-745) prints a structurally different, Steinmeyer-
shaped formula (metric, `mountingFactor * d^4/L^2 * 1e4` for buckling,
linear in `d` for critical speed) with its own worked numbers.
`thk-benchmark.ts` implements THK's formula as a genuinely separate
computation (not a wrapper around `math.ts`) and is tested in
`thk-benchmark.test.ts`:

- Independent method/tool: THK's own buckling and critical-speed formulas
  (`resolveThkBucklingLoad`, `resolveThkCriticalSpeed`)
- Case(s) compared: THK's own worked geometry (`d1 = 17.5 mm`,
  `ls = 1100 mm`) evaluated through both `thk-benchmark.ts` (reproducing
  THK's own `15,500 N` buckling and `2180`/`3294 min^-1` critical-speed
  results exactly) and `math.ts`'s Rockford-based functions for the
  equivalent fixed-fixed / fixed-supported geometry
- Result from independent method: `15,502 N` buckling (THK); `2184` and
  `3294 min^-1` critical speed (THK)
- MachineStudio result (`math.ts`, Rockford-based): `~29,992 N` buckling;
  `~2570 min^-1` critical speed for the equivalent geometry
- Difference and explanation: ratios of `0.52` (buckling) and `0.85`
  (critical speed) — same order of magnitude, not floating-point agreement.
  THK's own mounting-factor constants (`20` buckling, `15.1` critical speed)
  differ from Steinmeyer's published table for the identical nominal end
  conditions (`22.4`, `17.7`) by roughly 10-15%, and use an entirely
  different formula shape than Rockford's. This is a genuine, unresolved
  disagreement between three manufacturer sources on the exact
  buckling/critical-speed calibration constant for nominally identical end
  conditions — not a bug, not silently reconciled. `math.ts` keeps
  Rockford's own formula and coefficients because only Rockford's page
  supplied a worked example that formula shape reproduces exactly.

**3. Equivalent dynamic load — THK independent benchmark
(`lib/modules/ball-screw/0.1.0/thk-benchmark.ts`).** `math.ts` implements
Steinmeyer's single weighted-cube-mean formula, evaluated over every
duty-cycle phase directly. THK's own worked example uses a genuinely
different methodology for a bidirectional/reversing duty cycle: two
direction-specific averages, each normalized against the full round-trip
travel distance. `resolveThkDirectionalEquivalentLoad` (new) implements
THK's own method as a separate computation, tested in
`thk-benchmark.test.ts`:

- Independent method/tool: THK's own bidirectional-duty-cycle equivalent-
  load method (`resolveThkDirectionalEquivalentLoad`)
- Case(s) compared: THK's own six-phase worked scenario (the same
  WTF2040-2 example cited in Examples 4-6), evaluated through both
  `thk-benchmark.ts` (reproducing THK's own printed `225 N` exactly in both
  directions) and `math.ts`'s `resolveEquivalentDynamicLoad`, fed the
  mathematically equivalent per-phase `(time fraction, rotational speed,
  load)` triples derived from the same six phases
- Result from independent method: `225 N` (THK, both directions)
- MachineStudio result (`math.ts`): `~283.5 N` for the identical scenario
- Difference and explanation: a genuine, substantial disagreement (`~26%`),
  not a rounding difference — confirmed by a test that explicitly asserts
  the two values are not close, not just stated in a comment. This is a
  real, unresolved methodological question about how to aggregate a
  reversing duty cycle's equivalent load, not a bug in either
  implementation. `math.ts` is unchanged; `resolveThkDirectionalEquivalentLoad`'s
  own doc comment records the further open question THK's own example
  doesn't need to answer (what to do when the two directions' equivalent
  loads disagree, since THK's own scenario is direction-symmetric).

## Tolerances and Deviations

- The buckling safety margin (applied to the raw buckling load) is
  engineer-supplied per run rather than a single fixed constant: Steinmeyer
  states `0.5`; Rockford's own worked example applies `0.8` to the identical
  formula; neither is definitively authoritative
  (`context/modules/ball-screw/stage-2-contract.md` "Decisions" item 2).
- The static safety factor minimum is engineer-supplied per run rather than
  a single fixed constant: no manufacturer or standards-body minimum was
  confirmed by direct reading across two sourcing sessions
  (`stage-2-contract.md` "Decisions" item 1).
- The DN/manufacturer-speed-limit check evaluates only when the specific
  screw's own catalog data supplies it; it is not a formula this module
  derives (`stage-1-spec.md` item 9). THK's own catalog does print a
  DN-value formula (`N2 = 70,000/D`, reproduced in `thk-benchmark.ts`), but
  `70,000` reads as a series-specific constant, not a universal law.
- `resolveEquivalentDynamicLoad` sums one weighted-cube-mean over every
  supplied duty-cycle phase (Steinmeyer's published formula, evaluated
  directly). THK's own worked example instead splits a bidirectional/
  reversing duty cycle into two direction-specific averages, each
  normalized against the full round-trip travel distance rather than its
  own sub-total — a real procedural difference, not an input-mapping
  detail: feeding THK's own six phases through this kernel's formula gives
  `~283.5 N`, not THK's own printed `225 N`. See "Independent Method or Tool
  Comparison" item 3 above — both methods are now implemented and tested;
  neither is resolved into the other. `math.ts`'s own formula was not
  changed pending further evidence on which convention a released module
  should follow.
- Buckling/critical-speed: see "Independent Method or Tool Comparison"
  above — three sources (Rockford, Steinmeyer, THK) now disagree on the
  exact calibration constant for nominally identical end conditions.
  `math.ts` keeps Rockford's values; the discrepancy is recorded, not
  reconciled.

Every deviation above is also recorded in `lib/modules/ball-screw/0.1.0/
validation.ts`'s `deviations` array, which is the field a future sealed
`ValidationRecord` will actually carry.

## Unsupported Conditions

- `holding` and `emergency_stop` load cases are not implemented (only
  `normal`/`peak`).
- The rotating-nut / translating-screw arrangement is not supported (only
  rotating-screw / translating-nut).
- Only four end-support arrangements are supported; an intermediate-support
  (multi-span) arrangement is out of scope.
- A distance-basis dynamic load rating is rejected outright, not converted.
- No built-in static safety factor minimum or buckling safety margin — both
  must be supplied per run.
- No preload-dependent stiffness modeling beyond internal-friction/
  efficiency terms, no thermal derating, no structural compliance,
  backlash, or lubrication-regime modeling.
- No catalog search/ranking — this module checks a screw the engineer has
  already selected by size, lead, and end-support arrangement.
- The buckling/critical-speed calibration constants (Rockford's, as
  implemented) are one of at least three disagreeing manufacturer
  conventions for the same nominal end condition — see "Tolerances and
  Deviations."

## Supported Use Limits (Summary)

`ball-screw` 0.1.0 checks static safety, nominal life, buckling, and
critical speed for a single, already-selected ball-screw shaft on one
linear axis, under the `normal`/`peak` load cases only, for one of four
standard end-support arrangements using the screw's minor (root) diameter.
Drive torque, buckling, and critical speed are each corroborated by at
least one independent manufacturer source or computation; nominal life and
static safety factor are corroborated by a second manufacturer's worked
numbers. The static safety factor minimum and buckling safety margin have
no built-in default — both are required per-run inputs because published
guidance disagrees. It does not support holding/emergency-stop cases,
rotating-nut arrangements, distance-basis dynamic load ratings, or catalog
search/ranking. Released and registered as `ball-screw@0.1.0` 2026-08-12.
