## Purpose and Supported Applications

Given a horizontal belt conveyor's own drive-roller and idler-roller
geometry and mass, belt and carried-load mass, friction, mechanical
efficiency, and one commanded accelerate-to-speed motion event, computes
the required motor specifications a motor directly connected to the
drive-roller shaft (no gearbox) must meet: load torque, acceleration
torque, maximum momentary (starting) torque, required torque with an
engineer-supplied safety factor, operating speed, required power, total
reflected system inertia, and inertia ratio. It reports required-spec
values and pass/fail checks against engineer-supplied margins — "the
engineer takes the number to the catalog," the same scope
`ball-screw-motor-sizing@0.1.0` already established for the family. It
does not select a gearbox/coupling/drive/brake, model an inclined
conveyor, compute an effective (RMS) torque over a repeating duty cycle,
or match a motor catalog (ADR-0011 "Output scope").

## Validity Envelope and Assumptions

- One horizontal belt conveyor: a drive roller directly connected to the
  motor (`i = 1`, no gearbox — no gear-ratio port exists in this module's
  own input schema at all) and one idler roller, which may differ in
  diameter from the drive roller.
- One accelerate-to-speed event from standstill to a commanded target belt
  speed — not a repeating duty cycle; no deceleration-phase or effective
  (RMS) torque is computed.
- No incline, no thermal derating, no belt-slip check.
- Self-contained per ADR-0011 "Reuse policy": reproduces, rather than
  imports, Omron Corporation's and Oriental Motor Co., Ltd.'s own conveyor
  sizing methods. The genuine imports are `lib/engine/mechanics` (Unit
  6.1) for moment of inertia, `alpha = delta_omega/t`, and `Ta = J*alpha`.
- No servo-motor holding-torque formula, no motor catalog matching —
  `required_torque` and `required_power` are reported required-spec
  values, not pass/fail checks against a candidate motor.

## Reference Examples (Published Worked Examples)

### Example 1 — Oriental Motor Co., Ltd., "Belt and Pully" (p. F-8)

- Source: `jp.oriental_motor.general_catalog_motor_fan_sizing@f-tecref-2003-2004`,
  General Catalog Technical Reference, printed p. F-8
- Inputs: `m1=30 lb` (belt+work), `mu=0.3`, `D=4 in` (both rollers),
  `m2=35.27 oz` (both rollers, identical), `eta=0.9`, `V=7 in/s` (geared
  `i=50` in the source; this module's own `0.1.0` scope fixes `i=1` — the
  reference example itself validates the general formula upstream of the
  gearhead, not this module's own narrower direct-drive scope)
- Published/expected result: `F=144 oz` (`40.03 N`), `TL=320 oz-in`
  (`2.260 N*m`), `J1(both rollers)=141 oz-in^2`, `J2(belt+work)=
  1920 oz-in^2`, on-shaft total `J=2061 oz-in^2` (`0.03770 kg*m^2`),
  `NG=33.4 r/min`
- MachineStudio result: reproduced twice — at the kernel level
  (`math.test.ts` "end-to-end: Oriental Motor Co., Ltd.'s own 'Belt and
  Pully' worked example") and through `executeModule`
  (`oriental-motor-reference-examples.ts`/`.test.ts`)
- Difference: friction force and load torque within 5-6 decimal places
  (exact algebra); on-shaft inertia within 0.06% (the source's own
  3-4-significant-figure rounding); operating speed within the source's
  own printed rounding
- Tolerance applied and why: as above — accounts for the source's own
  printed rounding, not slack introduced to pass the test
- Pass / fail against tolerance: **pass** (for `load_torque`, on-shaft
  inertia, and `operating_speed`; `acceleration_torque`/
  `momentary_torque`/`required_torque` are explicitly not claimed as
  reproduced here — see "Tolerances and Deviations")

### Example 2 — Oriental Motor Co., Ltd., "Conveyor" (p. F-9)

- Source: same document, printed p. F-9
- Inputs: `D=4 in`, `m1=2.2 lb` (per roller, both identical), `m2=33 lb`
  (belt+work), `mu=0.3`, `eta=0.9` (geared `i=15` in the source; not this
  module's own scope, same reasoning as Example 1)
- Published/expected result: `F=9.9 lb`, `TL=22 lb-in` (`2.486 N*m`),
  `Jm1(single roller)=70.4 oz-in^2`, `Jm2(belt+work, as printed)=
  132 oz-in^2`, `JG=273 oz-in^2`
- MachineStudio result: `load_torque` reproduced through `executeModule`
  (`oriental-motor-reference-examples.ts`/`.test.ts`); `Jm1` reproduced
  separately at the kernel level (`math.test.ts`)
- Difference: `load_torque` within 4-5 decimal places (exact algebra);
  `Jm1` within 6 decimal places. `Jm2`/`JG` are **not** reproduced — see
  "Tolerances and Deviations"
- Tolerance applied and why: as above for the figures actually claimed
- Pass / fail against tolerance: **pass** (for `load_torque` and `Jm1`;
  `Jm2`/`JG` explicitly not claimed as reproduced — recorded as a
  disclosed source-internal inconsistency, not silently passed)

## Tolerances and Deviations

- Neither of Oriental Motor Co., Ltd.'s own two conveyor worked examples
  (pp. F-8-F-9) computes an acceleration-torque term. Both derive their
  own final required-torque figure directly from load (friction) torque
  alone, with a safety factor — p. F-8's own text: *"On a belt conveyor,
  the greatest torque is needed when starting the belt,"* referring to
  static-friction breakaway, not an inertial (`J*alpha`) acceleration
  term. This module's own registered parameter contract (registry
  `1.10.0`, already released and immutable) nonetheless defines
  `acceleration_torque`, `momentary_torque = acceleration_torque+
  load_torque`, and `required_torque = momentary_torque*Sf`, mirroring the
  already-registered `jp.oriental_motor.motor_sizing_calculations` web
  page's own general `TM=(TL+Ta)*Sf` shape. This module's own kernel
  therefore computes a real, nonzero `acceleration_torque` for both
  reference scenarios, using an engineer-supplied `acceleration_time`
  neither source states — a real, disclosed evidence gap for these three
  specific output ports, not a formula this project invented without any
  source basis. The `Ta = J*alpha` relationship itself is independently
  validated elsewhere: `lib/engine/mechanics`' own `torque.test.ts`
  (against Oriental Motor's own packaged r/min form) and
  `ball-screw-motor-sizing@0.1.0`'s own worked examples (against Omron's
  and THK's own worked examples, which do include a real acceleration
  phase).
- p. F-9's own printed `Jm2=132 oz-in^2` (belt+work inertia) is internally
  inconsistent with its own `Jm1=70.4 oz-in^2` (single-roller inertia)
  three lines earlier in the same worked example: `Jm1` correctly
  multiplies its own `lb` input by 16 to convert to `oz` before squaring
  the diameter (`2.2 lb * 16 = 35.2 oz`, matching `m2=35.27 oz` used
  identically in the separately-verified p. F-8 example), while `Jm2` uses
  its own `33 lb` input directly, without the same `*16` conversion
  (`33*(4/2)^2=132`, not `528*(4/2)^2=2112`). Recorded as a source-internal
  printing/arithmetic error, not resolved by this module (this environment
  cannot render a cleaner copy of this specific dense PDF page to
  double-check by eye) — this module's own kernel implements the
  physically correct, internally consistent formula throughout, and does
  not reproduce the printed `132` figure.

## Unsupported Conditions

- Any mechanism other than a direct-drive (`i = 1`), horizontal, two-roller
  belt conveyor — a geared/pulley-reduced drive of a rigid load is a
  different mechanism (`motor-sizing.belt-pulley-drive`, not yet built).
- Inclined conveyors — no source found gives an inclined-conveyor formula
  or worked example.
- A repeating duty cycle or effective (RMS) torque check.
- `acceleration_torque`/`momentary_torque`/`required_torque` against a
  real printed conveyor-specific figure — quantified above as a real,
  disclosed evidence gap (the formula itself is independently validated
  elsewhere).
- Motor catalog matching or part selection.
- No `linear-axis@1` or other guided-workflow role — this module is
  standalone in `0.1.0` (ADR-0011).

## Supported Use Limits (Summary)

One horizontal, direct-drive (`i=1`) belt conveyor, one drive roller and
one idler roller (possibly differing in diameter), one accelerate-to-speed
motion event. One required combined torque safety factor (`>= 1`) and one
required maximum inertia ratio, no built-in defaults. Validated against
two published worked examples from Oriental Motor Co., Ltd. (load torque,
on-shaft inertia, operating speed) plus one independent benchmark (Omron
Corporation's own structurally different combined inertia formula, proved
identical across 201 scenarios). `acceleration_torque`/`momentary_torque`/
`required_torque` are computed per an already-released, immutable
parameter contract, but not validated against a real printed
conveyor-specific figure — a disclosed evidence gap, not a code defect
(the underlying `Ta=J*alpha` relationship is independently validated
elsewhere). No motor catalog matching. Released and registered as
`direct-drive-conveyor-motor-sizing@0.1.0` 2026-08-13 — the second module
in the Motor Sizing Tool family (ADR-0011).
